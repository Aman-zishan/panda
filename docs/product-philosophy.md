# PANDA product philosophy

PANDA means **Presence-Aware Non-verbal Desk Assistant**. It is a calm physical desk
companion that should make the next useful action easier without asking for attention,
opening a feed or duplicating a phone.

## Core rule

**PANDA only does things that become better because they are physical, immediate or
present at the desk.**

If a phone already does a job well through a screen, PANDA should hand that job to the
companion app rather than recreate it on the device.

## What PANDA should do

- Hear a quick spoken thought when reaching for a phone would interrupt work.
- Give a short, unmistakable confirmation through sound, light, posture or one glance
  at the display.
- Let a person start, pause, snooze or acknowledge with one knob action.
- Make focus and breaks visible through its small world and physical presence.
- Notice a person arriving at or leaving the desk, later in the project.
- Nudge at the right moment, then become quiet again.

## What belongs in the companion app

- Full transcripts and editing.
- Lists of reminders, notes and to-dos.
- History, search, settings and account controls.
- Calendar detail and any information that needs reading.
- Setup, troubleshooting and advanced configuration.

## What PANDA must not become

- A dashboard with scrolling lists.
- A chat screen or a text-first assistant.
- A stream of notifications competing for attention.
- A keyboard, touch interface or miniature phone.
- A replacement for the phone's calendar, notes app or task manager.
- A device that speaks long human-language replies.

## Interaction principles

### One glance, one action

The display should answer only: *what is happening now?* The knob should make the
next action obvious. If an interaction needs several choices, it belongs in the app.

### Communicate through character

Use small sounds, light, head posture and the tiny world before using text. Text is for
a brief confirmation only, such as the captured reminder time.

### Be quiet by default

PANDA should not demand attention. It waits, gives one useful nudge, and then returns
to calm. It should remain silent during meetings and respect a physical microphone mute.

### Make the physical form matter

Every feature on the device should benefit from being always present on the desk:
voice capture, a knob, a glanceable state, sound, presence sensing or posture. If it
does not benefit from physical presence, put it in the app or remove it.

### Never punish

The tiny world may wilt when focus or breaks slip, but it never dies, shames or creates
an obligation. It should recover within a day.

## Feature check

Before adding a device feature, answer these questions:

1. Does a phone already do this better as a screen task?
2. Does the physical desk presence make this faster, calmer or more meaningful?
3. Can the result be understood in one glance, one sound or one knob action?
4. Does it reduce distraction rather than create another place to check?
5. Would PANDA still feel calm if this happened several times a day?

If the answer to questions 2–4 is no, do not add the feature to the device.
