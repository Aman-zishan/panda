# Tiny-world rules

Draft for [#8](https://github.com/Aman-zishan/panda/issues/8). Included in Basic and
Connect, with local state saved across restarts.

A small landscape and its creature quietly reflect focus and rest. They are company
at the desk, not another thing to look after. No feeding, scores, streaks or daily chores.

## What you see

| What happens | How the world responds |
| --- | --- |
| Idle | The creature rests in a calm landscape. Nothing demands attention. |
| Focus starts | The creature settles into a focused pose. A simple progress indicator and remaining time make the session clear. |
| Focus is paused | Progress stops; the creature rests. Nothing is lost. |
| Focus ends early | Return to idle without a failure animation or loss of previous growth. |
| Focus completes | A small plant grows or blooms once. A brief celebration gives way to a resting scene, inviting a break. |
| Completion is acknowledged or snoozed | Clear the prompt or postpone it using the knob rules. The completed session's growth remains. |
| A voice request or reminder appears | Brief feedback takes priority, then the world returns. Captures and acknowledgements do not earn growth. |
| PANDA restarts or loses its connection | Restore saved growth. No decay or catch-up penalties for time offline or powered off. |

Growth stays within one small scene. Once it is fully grown, completed sessions can
briefly brighten it without adding more objects, levels or rewards to collect.
The creature never dies, becomes ill or asks to be rescued.

## Breaks and recovery

**Stage 1:** a resting scene suggests a pause after focus. Acknowledging a prompt is
not proof of taking a break. Without presence sensing, PANDA does not judge desk
time, skipped breaks or absence, and the world does not wilt.

**With presence sensing:** being away lets the creature rest. Returning brings a
small welcome. A deferred break nudge may gently droop a plant, but never remove
growth or make the creature distressed. Quiet mode also suppresses these attention
cues. Presence alone is not a measure of productivity.

A detected break or the next completed focus session restores the plant. Any droop
also clears within a day without requiring action. Sensor uncertainty must not
cause wilting. Break timing and the duration that counts as a break belong in the
focus-experience requirements; no head movement or sensor is needed for Stage 1.

## Keep it glanceable

Use distinct poses and shapes that work on the small monochrome display. Keep most
of the scene still; animation is brief and tied to a meaningful change. No scrolling
text, world menus, separate alerts from the creature or flashing to attract attention.

Before closing this ticket, review whether someone can recognise focus, pause,
completion and rest without a manual, and whether any behaviour feels like an
obligation. Validate legibility and distraction on the real display later.
